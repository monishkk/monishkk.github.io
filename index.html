03 Quantitative Research

Aug 2026 – Present

Python · pandas · NumPy · SciPy · pytest · GitHub Actions

Cross-Sectional ETF Momentum

Reproducible research platform · github.com/monishkk/quant-research-platform

  1. Built a config-driven Python research platform spanning an automated data pipeline (ingestion, validation, caching, provenance) through signal generation, portfolio simulation, performance attribution, and reporting across 9 US ETFs over 17.9 years (4,502 trading days); one command reproduces every figure, table, and a 10-section research report.
  2. Engineered against standard backtest failure modes — T+1 execution lag, self-financing accounting with weight drift, two-way turnover costs, parameters fixed before results were inspected — validated by 197 unit and regression tests in CI across two Python versions, including look-ahead checks that mutate future prices and assert historical signals are unchanged.
  3. Determined the strategy’s +0.111 Sharpe advantage over SPY was not statistically significant (stationary block bootstrap p = 0.53; Deflated Sharpe 0.70 vs. a 192-trial noise hurdle of 0.40) and traced the entire edge to one year via leave-one-year-out analysis — excluding 2008 the advantage reverses to −0.03.
  4. Isolated the surviving effect with a Monte Carlo null of 300 randomized 3-of-9 rotations: the ranking outperformed all 300 (p < 0.01) and held when random portfolios were given zero transaction costs, separating selection skill from turnover; reported Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, maximum drawdown, VaR/CVaR, and HAC-corrected alpha regression.

Note — on the headline result

The negative finding is the finding. The platform was built to be capable of failing to confirm its own hypothesis, and it did: once multiple-testing and turnover costs are accounted for, the momentum edge does not survive. Reporting that honestly is the point of the work.
